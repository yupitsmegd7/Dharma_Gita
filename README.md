## How Dharma helps

A verse can be memorable without being easy to understand or apply. Dharma brings the passage, its meaning, its context and a small practical reflection into one reading space.

It is designed for students facing pressure, readers beginning their study of scripture, and anyone who wants to examine their reactions and choices more carefully. You can start with a verse—or with something on your mind.

| When you are… | Open… | What you can do |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Worried about an exam, interview or uncertain result | **Ask the Gita** | Follow a reading sequence about effort, steadiness and the next responsible action. |
| Struggling with anger or a defensive response | **Ask the Gita** | Read about the escalation of anger and reflect on truthful, considerate speech before responding. |
| Distracted, procrastinating or repeatedly scrolling | **Daily practice** | Name a trigger, choose a small replacement action and record seven practice sessions. |
| Looking for a quiet moment of reflection | **Mantras** | Explore complete selected passages by feeling or situation, with Sanskrit text and Hindi and English meanings. |
| Curious about the choices made by epic characters | **Lives & lessons** | Consider loyalty, courage, responsibility and discernment through specific episodes. |
| Unsure where a teaching or quotation comes from | **The Gita / Sources** | Check its chapter, verse, translator and linked source instead of relying on an isolated quotation. |

### A question becomes a reading path

For a concern such as **“I am afraid of failing my exam,”** the curated *Fear of failure* path presents:

1. **Gita 2.47 — Separate action from outcome.** Identify the effort that is yours to make.
2. **Gita 2.48 — Meet the result with steadiness.** Reflect on your response to success and failure.
3. **Gita 3.8 — Choose the next action.** Return to something responsible and concrete.

The website explains why the verses appear in that order. Its suggested practice is to write down one action you can complete today and one result you will stop trying to control.

This sequence is an editorial application of the passages. The question feature uses **eight curated themes and local keyword matching**; it does not generate new scripture or claim to answer every possible question.

## Texts and trust

The original text, an attributed translation and a present-day application are different things. Dharma identifies those layers so readers can compare them.

| Content | Source or editorial approach |
| --- | --- |
| **Gita Sanskrit and translations** | Digitized corpus from [gita/gita](https://github.com/gita/gita), with verse-level links to [IIT Kanpur's Gita Supersite](https://www.gitasupersite.iitk.ac.in/). |
| **English Gita translation** | Attributed to **Swami Sivananda**. |
| **Hindi Gita translation** | Attributed to **Swami Ramsukhdas**, except 13.1, which uses **Swami Tejomayananda** because the Ramsukhdas field has no translation for that entry. |
| **Mahabharata episodes and formations** | **K. M. Ganguli's 1883–1896 translation**, with book and section references, available through the [Internet Sacred Text Archive](https://sacred-texts.com/hin/maha/index.htm) and SriPedia. |
| **Ramayana episodes** | Kanda and sarga references through [IIT Kanpur's Valmiki Ramayanam](https://www.valmiki.iitk.ac.in/). |
| **Scriptural-family overview** | D. C. Rao / Hindu American Foundation's introductory guide, identified as a modern overview. |
| **Mantra meanings and daily reflections** | Plain-language editorial Hindi and English renderings, with source links and variant or attribution notes where applicable. |
| **Modern examples and habit exercises** | Editorial suggestions for reflection and practice, not quotations claimed to appear in scripture. |

<details>
<summary><strong>Why 701 Gita entries, and what does “complete mantra” mean?</strong></summary>

The familiar Gita count is **700 verses**. This corpus includes Arjuna's opening question at **13.1**, followed by Krishna's response at **13.2**. Editions that omit the question number the later verses of chapter 13 one lower. The reader explains this difference.

A **complete passage** in the Mantras section means the complete selected mantra, prayer or verse unit—not a half-verse. A verse taken from a longer hymn is labelled with its location; it is not presented as the entire hymn.

The collection contains **6 Vedic mantras, 11 Upanishadic mantras or invocations, 36 traditional prayer verses and 1 Gita verse used in prayer**. These categories avoid attributing every devotional verse to the Vedas. Sanskrit is displayed without Vedic pitch accents, and IAST Roman text is a transliteration, not an English translation.

</details>

<details>
<summary><strong>Formation diagrams, artwork and limits of interpretation</strong></summary>

The formation catalogue follows Ganguli's **Bhishma, Drona, Karna and Shalya** books. It distinguishes named formations, components of compound arrays and descriptive deployments. Occurrences used by both armies retain separate references and positions.

Diagrams are **modern teaching schematics**, not verified battlefield maps or simulations. Where a passage names a form without describing its geometry—such as Madhyama or the Asura/Pishacha/Rakshasa styles—the site does not invent a plan. All-facing and ocean-like descriptions are distinguished from formally named arrays.

The chariot painting, chakra and ornamental frieze are **contemporary AI-generated artwork**, not medieval artifacts.

Hindu texts and traditions contain differences. Sources establish what a particular text or translator says; they do not establish every narrative event as independently verified history. Digitization errors may remain, and the collection has not received a complete scholarly proofreading.

</details>

## Run locally

This is a **static HTML, CSS and JavaScript website**. No package installation, backend or AI API key is required.

```sh
git clone https://github.com/yupitsmegd7/Dharma_Gita.git
cd Dharma_Gita
python -m http.server 8000 --directory dist
```

Open **[http://localhost:8000](http://localhost:8000)** in your browser.

On Windows, use `py` instead of `python` if needed. Serve the folder over HTTP; opening `index.html` directly through `file://` will prevent the bundled verse data from loading correctly.

**Static serving directory:** `dist/` · **Build step:** none

## How the app works

| Layer | Implementation |
| --- | --- |
| **Interface** | Semantic HTML and responsive CSS, with local artwork and CSS motion. |
| **Navigation and interaction** | Vanilla JavaScript and hash-based routes. |
| **Scripture data** | Bundled JSON and JavaScript collections with reference metadata. |
| **Question matching** | Local keyword/theme matching against curated reading paths. |
| **Saved practice** | Browser `localStorage` for the habit record and reading preferences. |
| **Optional integration** | WebMCP tool registration when a supporting browser exposes it. |

Practice records stay in the browser where they were created; they do not sync between devices. Questions are matched locally and are not sent to an AI service.

## Verification and provenance

Existing structural and DOM checks cover all **701 Gita entry renders**, all **54 mantra passages**, language modes, search, reading-path selection, habit saving/editing, formation filters, keyboard boundaries and page-turn cleanup. They also check both chakra placements, contact targets and reduced-motion behavior.

These checks do not establish pixel-perfect browser layout or native WebMCP compatibility. Live browser layout and native WebMCP-context validation were unavailable in the static preview environment.
